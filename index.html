🫁 How do I calculate breaths per minute? ＋
1

Sit quietly and relax for a few minutes before measuring.

2

Watch your chest or abdomen rise and fall. One rise + one fall = 1 breath.

3

Count the number of breaths for 60 seconds. Try to breathe normally and do not intentionally change your breathing.

Breathing rate= breaths counted in 60 sExample: 16 breaths in 60 seconds = 16 breaths/min

Quick method: count for 30 seconds and multiply by 2. A full 60-second count is preferred when the breathing rhythm is irregular.

How the website calculates it

First, the project's heart-rate-ratio equation estimates VO₂ in mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹. The value is converted to L/min using the participant's body mass. Energy is then calculated using VO₂ × 5 kcal/L and converted beside it using 1 kcal = 4.184 kJ.

06 / HEALTH SCORE METHODOLOGY

How does one score happen?

Five category scores (each out of 100) are combined using a fixed equal weightage. Each category contributes 20% to the Health Score.

Health Score = Σ (category score × 20% each)
See the category scoring rules ＋

Nutrition: 85% comes from the project food-balance checklist and 15% from water intake (up to 8 × 250 mL/day). The food-balance score uses both food-group coverage and the user's simple quantity/times-eaten entries across carbohydrate, protein, vegetables, fruits, dairy/alternatives and healthy fats. Quantities are capped against project targets for scoring, so repeatedly eating one food cannot increase the score without limit. The dashboard also shows an estimated calorie total using typical per-portion values for each food. This is an educational estimate only because portion size, recipe, oil, sauces and cooking method can substantially change the true calorie value; Stair activity: climbs relative to the project reference of 10/day, with staircase location recorded for context; Cardiovascular: 50% resting pulse and 50% resting breathing rate, using the reference bands adopted by this project; the after-climb pulse is visualised but is not penalised in the score; BMI: adult screening-range reference of 18.5–24.9 (for adults 20+ only); Stress: the five-question stress check is converted into a 1–10 level and then reversed into the category score. All five category scores are capped at 100.

The Health Score is a project-defined wellness index. Its equal weightage and cut-offs are used for this Integrated STEM project and must not be interpreted as medical diagnostic thresholds.

✳ Health information notice

The Health Dashboard is an Integrated STEM wellness tool and does not provide medical diagnosis, treatment, or emergency advice. The pulse trace is a mathematical visualisation rather than a measured ECG. Staircase pulse values are drawn from the project dataset or clearly labelled estimates. BMI categories shown are adult screening references; BMI-for-age should be used for younger users. If a health measurement or symptom concerns you, seek appropriate medical advice.

Evidence & project inputs ＋

Project-provided data: the three Group 6 staircase geometry/pulse measurements were taken from PROJECT STEM.docx; additional campus staircase locations and angles come from the GROUP HEALTH DASHBOARD sheet; pulse values for those additional stairs are model estimates from the three measured Group 6 calibration stairs; formulas f = BPM/60, T = 1/f and ω = 2πf were adapted from its physics methodology. External reference points: WHO healthy diet (≥400 g fruit/vegetables/day for ages over 10); AHA adult resting heart rate information; CDC adult BMI categories (age 20+). Other scoring choices are defined by the project methodology and are not clinical thresholds.
